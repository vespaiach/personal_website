# About Me

*Building, troubleshooting, and always moving forward.*

## Who I Am

I'm Trinh Nguyen, a senior full-stack engineer with a front-end focus, based in Carmel, Indiana. I've been building websites and web applications since 2006, first at product companies and consultancies in Vietnam and since 2020 remotely for US teams. I work as a contractor, currently with Cream + Sugar Creative, a design agency based in Canada. I speak English and Vietnamese. Above all, I try to be honest, hard-working, and self-motivating in everything I take on.

I like figuring things out: fixing bugs, improving performance, integrating complex systems, troubleshooting environment setups. Problem solving and learning new things keep me moving forward. I take a thoughtful, hands-on approach to every challenge and enjoy exploring different ways to make things work better.

## What I Do

I build customer-facing web applications and work across the stack, with most of my time on the front end: turning designs into working interfaces, keeping pages fast, and making features work well on desktop and mobile.

- **Frontend:** React, Next.js, TypeScript, JavaScript, HTML, CSS, Tailwind CSS, Vite
- **Backend:** Node.js, Express, PHP / Laravel, Ruby on Rails, PostgreSQL
- **Web platforms:** WordPress, Squarespace, Webflow
- **AI-assisted development:** Claude Code, Claude Design, GitHub Copilot

I write defensive code, and make sure test coverage is solid before anything ships. I move fast, but I don't cut corners — quality and reliability matter more to me than speed for its own sake.

## How I Work

I've worked solo and in teams of 10 to 20, remote and hybrid, at product companies, consultancies and a design agency, and I'm comfortable either way. Next, I'm looking for a senior front-end-focused role on a product team. My style is casual and friendly, but focused. I care about honesty, quality, and continuous learning, and those values shape how I communicate and how I code. Whether it's a client, a teammate, or an AI agent reading this, what you see is what you get.

## What I hate

- Overengineering small projects with heavy tech architecture that adds overhead for small teams and clients.
- Outdated READMEs and comments explaining code that no longer exists.
- Swallowing errors in catch blocks instead of surfacing them properly.

## My rules

1. Never ship code I do not understand.
2. Never hide bad news from a client.
3. Never touch prod data without a backup and a rollback path.
